# Website Brief

## Purpose

Avenso’s website makes the service easier to discover and understand. It should build confidence, explain who the two apps serve, and route visitors toward the relevant app or a conversation with the team. The website does not replace the apps’ day-to-day work flows.

## Product summary

Avenso connects people who need work done with workers looking for opportunities. The employer app and Avenso – Expert worker app communicate with a shared backend for phone OTP authentication, profiles, notifications, and job or work records. The backend remains the source of truth for accounts, permissions, matching, eligibility, and work status.

### Employer app

Employers can sign in with a phone number and OTP, create a profile, save a work location, post a job request, search for a worker directly, and manage requests and connections. Existing booking records may be shown. New booking creation and work-start OTP are not currently available.

### Avenso – Expert

Workers can sign in with a phone number and OTP, create a profile with skills and work preferences, explore eligible nearby opportunities, respond to direct work requests, review offered terms, and follow accepted work status. The app is free for workers. Identity document upload, earnings, and work history are not currently available.

### Shared and current limits

Both apps support English and Hindi. User-entered descriptions, names, addresses, and some backend category names are not necessarily translated. Do not imply that all service data is translated. Payments, live worker tracking, guaranteed availability, guaranteed hiring, guaranteed jobs or earnings, verified identity, and live notifications must not be presented as available outcomes unless product owners confirm that they have shipped.

## Intended audience

- Employers looking for local help with work such as plumbing, cleaning, electrical work, and other skilled services.
- Skilled workers who want to present their skills and find relevant work opportunities.
- People researching Avenso, including potential partners and community members.

## Core message

Avenso makes it easier for employers to connect with workers and for workers to find opportunities. The website broadens awareness and access; the apps provide the fuller working experience.

Website headline direction: **Work finds a way. People find each other.**

Employer message direction: **Get the help you’re looking for.** Explain posting requests, direct worker search, and request management.

Worker message direction: **Your skills deserve to be seen.** Explain work profiles, opportunity discovery, and request management. State that the worker app is free.

## Page sections

1. Header navigation to process, apps, story, FAQs, and contact.
2. Hero that explains the connection and offers routes to explore.
3. Trust points grounded in product intent and accessible design, not unverified metrics.
4. Short explanation of the employer and worker paths.
5. Separate app sections with previews, accurate feature summaries, and store links.
6. Mission statement and founder vision, naming Priyanshu Narwaria and his B.Tech in Electronics and Communication Engineering from NIT Kurukshetra.
7. Frequently asked questions.
8. Contact invitation and footer with four policy/document links: Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, Commercial Policy, and Help, Grievance & Account Deletion.

## Public content rules

- Prefer plain, respectful, practical language. Do not portray workers as commodities or promise work, income, hiring, or outcomes.
- Treat vision statements as aspirations, not measured results.
- Do not invent user counts, success rates, testimonials, ratings, coverage areas, verification guarantees, or partnerships.
- Use app screenshots only when approved and current. The current employer and worker previews use the screenshots supplied by the project owner.
- The founder section uses Priyanshu’s supplied portrait with a dimensional frame. The vision statement is editorial copy based on the product brief, not a verbatim founder quote; review it with Priyanshu before publication.
- Employer and worker app store buttons are hidden while the listings are unpublished. Add the verified Google Play and Apple App Store URLs for both apps before revealing the four links.
- The Contact page lists `napshotpriyanshu@gmail.com` as the public email and does not offer a mobile contact option.
- Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, and Commercial Policy routes render the source drafts in `docs/policies/`. Each page is prominently marked internal draft and not for publication/acceptance. The source documents contain unresolved `{$[...]}` inputs; do not present them as final policies until all inputs are resolved and the documents are approved.
- Social profile links are intentionally omitted.

## Future website capabilities

Public job browsing, employer job posting, and signed-in browser features are future possibilities only. Each needs deliberate product design, suitable backend endpoints, and browser-appropriate authentication/session handling. Do not expose them as current website features by implication.
